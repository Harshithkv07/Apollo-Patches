Apollo 17, the last Apollo mission to the Moon, lifted off at 12:33 a.m. EST on 7 December 1972. Gene Cernan and Harrison Schmitt landed *Challenger* in the Taurus-Littrow valley while Ron Evans orbited in *America*. They splashed down on 19 December.

The artist Robert T. McCall designed the emblem with the astronauts. The face is the Apollo Belvedere, the ancient statue of the sun god in the Vatican. Behind it flies an American eagle of contemporary design, with a wing made of the red bars of the U.S. flag and three white stars for the three crewmen.

NASA's official description reads almost like a design brief. The eagle's wing overlaps the Moon to suggest a world people had visited and, in that sense, conquered. The eagle's thrust and Apollo's gaze both point right, toward Saturn and a spiral galaxy, implying that people's goals in space would someday include the planets and perhaps the stars. The colors are the flag's red, white and blue, plus gold for "the golden age of space flight" this landing was meant to begin.
