Apollo 13 launched on 11 April 1970 to make the third lunar landing. Two days out, an oxygen tank in the service module exploded. The crew powered down *Odyssey*, used the lunar module *Aquarius* as a lifeboat, swung around the Moon without landing and splashed down on 17 April.

Jack Swigert flew as command module pilot. He had replaced Ken Mattingly days before launch, after Mattingly was exposed to German measles.

Jim Lovell started the emblem with an image of Apollo, the sun god, driving his chariot across the sky. New York artist Lumen Martin Winter turned it into three galloping horses, close relatives of the horses in a sky-crossing mural he had painted for the St. Regis Hotel in New York.

The motto takes the place of crew names. That makes Apollo 13 one of only two Apollo patches without them; the other is Apollo 11. So the patch needed no change when Swigert joined the crew. *Ex Luna, Scientia* means "From the Moon, knowledge." It is often said to echo the motto of the U.S. Naval Academy, Lovell's alma mater: *Ex Scientia Tridens*, "From knowledge, sea power."
