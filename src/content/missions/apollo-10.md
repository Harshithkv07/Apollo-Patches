Apollo 10 did everything a landing mission would do except the final descent to the surface. Tom Stafford, John Young and Gene Cernan launched on 18 May 1969. On 22 May, Stafford and Cernan took the lunar module, *Snoopy*, down to 47,400 feet above the Moon, then rejoined Young in the command module, *Charlie Brown*. They splashed down on 26 May.

According to collectSPACE, the crew asked Allen Stevens of North American Rockwell to design their insignia. NASA says the shield shape was based more on mechanics than on mission goals. The three-dimensional Roman numeral X identifies the mission and seems to sit on the Moon. The command module circles above while the lunar module makes its low pass, with Earth in the background.

Apollo 10 didn't land. But in NASA's reading, the prominence of the X showed that the mission would make a significant contribution to the program.
